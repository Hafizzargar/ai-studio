# Introduction
Welcome to this short lesson on the water cycle. Water is always moving around our planet. It rises into the sky, falls back to the ground, and flows to the sea again. This movement is called the water cycle.

# Evaporation
The sun heats water in oceans, lakes and rivers. Some of this liquid water turns into water vapour, which is an invisible gas. This process is called evaporation. Plants also release water vapour through their leaves, which is known as transpiration.

# Condensation
As water vapour rises, it cools. Cool vapour changes back into tiny droplets of liquid water. This is called condensation. Billions of droplets gather together to form clouds.

# Precipitation
When droplets in a cloud grow heavy, they fall to the ground. This is called precipitation. It can fall as rain, snow, sleet or hail, depending on the temperature of the air.

# Collection
Rain and melting snow flow into streams and rivers. Some water soaks into the ground and becomes groundwater. Most of it eventually returns to the ocean, and the cycle begins again.

# Summary
To remember the water cycle, think of four steps: evaporation, condensation, precipitation and collection. Together they keep fresh water moving around the world, and they make life on Earth possible.
